The symbols of the results

A clear visual language for orientation, impact, and interplay

A map, not a judgement

The symbols make results easier to read. They show which orientation leads, which perspective complements it, and when a result is balanced. No symbol is better or worse than another.

Individual symbols and equally weighted combinations are shown at full size. For ranked results, the tables use the moderate rendering: the complementary element appears at 75 percent. FIX uses 60 percent and CLOSE uses 90 percent. Read more about this visual language under Measuring personality.

1. Orientation / Axisprint

X and Y form the basic coordinate system. They show which orientation is more prominent in the result. In a balanced result, both axes remain equally visible.

X · horizontal orientation Y · vertical orientation
Key Symbol Description
X The horizontal orientation leads; Y complements the result.
Y The vertical orientation leads; X complements the result.
XY Both axes are balanced; neither orientation dominates.

2. Priorities / Drivecode

The four half-axes form an inner compass. They do not represent rigid types, but different forms of orientation. In a combined symbol, the leading direction is shown at full size while the runner-up remains visible as a smaller, filled silhouette.

W · meaning and connection N · development and will E · agency and impact S · stability and reliability
Key Symbol Description
W Meaning and connection set the direction of this result.
N Development and will set the direction of this result.
E Agency and impact set the direction of this result.
S Stability and reliability set the direction of this result.
EW Agency and impact lead; meaning and connection complement the result as runner-up.
NS Development and will lead; stability and reliability complement the result as runner-up.

3. Impact spaces / Impactspace

Where orientations meet, four impact spaces emerge. The chess pieces distil each perspective into a recognisable image. They describe viewpoints and preferences, not fixed personality types.

Q1 · Frontier Q2 · Spark Q3 · Anchor Q4 · Calm
Key Symbol Description
Q1 Frontier opens the view to curiosity, possibilities, and unconventional paths.
Q2 Spark gives ideas energy, direction, and the power to become action.
Q3 Anchor creates clarity, structure, and reliable foundations.
Q4 Calm stands for reflection, experience, and recognising connections.
Q12 Frontier and Spark combine a spirit of discovery with the energy to turn ideas into impact.
Q14 Frontier and Calm combine openness to new paths with thoughtful interpretation.
Q23 Spark and Anchor combine creative agency with structure and reliability.
Q34 Anchor and Calm combine dependable order with experience and reflection.
Q0 Balance denotes an even result in which no impact space dominates.

4. Role profiles / Roleprofile

A role profile combines the strongest impact space with the second strongest. The first digit identifies the leading perspective and the second the complementary one. The image therefore shows an interplay, not a fixed category. R0 represents a balanced result without ranking.

Key Symbol Description
R12 Frontier leads with curiosity and new perspectives; Spark adds agency and the power to act.
R14 Frontier leads with openness to new paths; Calm adds reflection and a sense of connections.
R21 Spark leads with agency and the power to act; Frontier adds curiosity and new perspectives.
R23 Spark leads with drive and impact; Anchor adds structure and reliability.
R32 Anchor leads with structure and reliability; Spark adds energy and the power to act.
R34 Anchor leads with clarity and dependable order; Calm adds experience and reflection.
R41 Calm leads with reflection and connected thinking; Frontier adds openness to new paths.
R43 Calm leads with experience and reflection; Anchor adds clarity and reliable structure.
R0 Balance represents an even Roleprofile without a leading impact space.
